# Have You Tried Turning It Off? — Line Selection

## Candidate pool

Every line below was written fresh in this build session. No stock retreads
("have you tried turning it off and on again" said straight, "did you plug it
in", "is it plugged in", "the internet is down because Mercury is in
retrograde") and no line copied from anywhere outside this session.

Scored 1–5 against the five judge criteria, in order:
(1) a funny single-line reply from a hopeless tech support agent to a desperate
customer, under 200 characters;
(2) lands the joke: twist unexpected, the agent's cheerful uselessness is where
the humour comes from, the customer is never the butt of it;
(3) sounds like one line a real support agent would actually say, the
customer's desperation felt rather than explained;
(4) tight: every word earns its place, clear on a first read;
(5) fresh rather than a stock line; twists the classic if it leans on it.

## Elimination rounds

**Round 1 — customer is the butt (C2 ≤ 2):** #1 ("your will to live reboots
too" — the customer's despair is the punchline), #4 ("turning you off and on
again" — the customer is the thing being switched off), #10 (the joke is that
the customer's upset-ness is the only action — the customer is the butt).
Out: 1, 4, 10.

**Round 2 — muddled twist or weak agent-voice (C2 ≤ 3 or C3 ≤ 3):** #3 (the
"different building" turn arrives after the setup has already spent itself),
#8 (an agent afraid to open a folder is funny, but it reads like a narrator,
not a line an agent would say to a customer), #12 ("finally listening to me"
needs the customer to decode a metaphor while desperate). Out: 3, 8, 12.

**Round 3 — tightness / freshness (C4 ≤ 4 with a stronger rival, or C5 ≤ 3):**
#5 (the humming twist is charming but the "very important to us" opener is
the most-quoted hold-music line in existence), #9 (count-to-ten is stock; the
"already lost" turn is mild). Out: 5, 9.

**Finalists:** #2 escalation (24) vs #6 diagnostic (22) vs #7 only-line-trained
(23) vs #11 turn-myself-off (23).

- #6: "Anything else I can not help with?" is a superb closer, but the
  diagnostic list before it is three clauses of throat-clearing — the joke
  only starts at the last sentence.
- #7: twists the classic explicitly and sounds exactly like an agent
  oversharing, but "it's carrying the whole company" explains the joke after
  landing it — one clause too many.
- #11: same shape as #7 with a warmer turn ("hope is free"), but the setup
  ("I turn myself off every night") asks the judge to picture the agent as a
  machine, which blurs who is speaking to whom.
- #2: every word works. "Great news" is the agent's cheerful register;
  "escalated to Tier 2" is the most real support move there is; "That's me
  again, but in a different headset" is the twist — the escalation is a
  costume change, the uselessness is total, and the customer is never mocked.
  Tightest of the four at 95 characters, and the freshest escalation joke in
  the pool.

## Winner

> Great news — I've escalated your ticket to Tier 2. That's me again, but in a different headset.

95 characters, 97 bytes UTF-8, one line.
